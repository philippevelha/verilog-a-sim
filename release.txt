verilog-a-sim — release log
===========================

Versioning, from 0.9.0 onward:

  * A feature, or anything that changes behaviour, bumps the patch:
    0.9.0 -> 0.9.1 -> 0.9.2 -> ...
  * A pure fix, or docs/process with no behaviour change, appends a build digit:
    0.9.8 -> 0.9.8+1 -> 0.9.8+2. Spelled `+N` and not `0.9.8.1` because Cargo versions
    are semver and a fourth numeric component is rejected outright (verified: `cargo
    metadata` refuses 0.9.7.1 and accepts 0.9.7+1).
  * Each bump gets a dated entry below with a sentence or two saying what changed.
  * 1.0.0 was reached on 2026-09-17, when the last "Road to 1.0" item (the list at the
    bottom, kept as the record of what it took) closed. From 1.0 on the same shape applies
    one level up: a feature bumps the minor, a fix the patch.
  * The version lives in the workspace Cargo.toml (`[workspace.package] version`);
    every crate inherits it with `version.workspace = true`. Bump both together —
    the number in this file and the one in Cargo.toml must always agree.

Newest first.

1.26.0 - 2026-09-26
-------------------
**Transient steps use the block-triangular solve, with a large block factored by `faer` on its
own** (`docs/proposals/transient-block-solve.md`, option A). `va_core::btf` no longer refuses a
matrix with a block over `MAX_BLOCK` (64): such a block gets its own pattern and `faer` solver,
cached with the plan, and the rest is dense or substitution as before. A block holding ≥ 90% of
the unknowns (`WHOLE_MATRIX_FRACTION`) still sends the whole matrix to `faer`, so a fully coupled
circuit keeps its exact path. `TranConfig::btf` (set from `VA_BTF`, default on) switches the
integrator's solves onto it; `SparseLu` now holds its block solver boxed.

  * **Speed:** c432's 1 ns transient (`c432_tran.net`), 8 threads, mains power: **1 300.4 s →
    877.9 s (1.48×)**; the solve had been 45% of the run at 39.6 ms per solve.
  * **Answers:** `deck-diff`, 1.24.0 vs 1.26.0: with `VA_BTF=off` 72 of 72 identical; defaults
    71 of 72 — c17's transient moves (13 462 → 13 486 steps, node voltages RMS ≤ 5.5e-5 of
    scale, edges ≤ 8.4 fs). c432's transient keeps its 2 524 timepoints, node voltages within
    7.2e-7 of scale. DC decks do not move. `validate` 28/28, errors unchanged.

**Limitations:** serial; a union that grows re-runs the large block's symbolic analysis; c17-size
transients gain nothing measurable (their solve is 0.1–0.2 ms).

Measured at this version: `cargo test --workspace` 907 passed (+1); `validate` 28/28, errors unchanged;

1.24.0 - 2026-09-26
-------------------
**The DC rescue walks its `gmin` ramp adaptively** (`docs/proposals/adaptive-gmin.md`). Same
ends as before — 1e-3 S first, exactly 0 last — and the same starting step (1/30 of the ramp),
but a stage that converges in ≤ 8 Newton iterations doubles the next step (up to a quarter of the
ramp), and a stage that fails is retried from the last converged point with half the step; after
three halvings in a row the solve fails with that stage's error. The fixed ramp had no back-off:
one failed stage ended the ladder.

  * `NewtonConfig::gmin_adaptive` (default `false`, so a caller asking for `gmin_steps` still
    gets equal stages); `dc::with_gmin_rescue` switches it on for every ladder tier.
    `convergence::gmin_at` gives the ramp at any position. The schedule is a function of a stage
    closure, unit-tested on its own: growth, back-off and retry from the same point, the
    unshunted end, and failure at the step floor; plus the 20-diode chain agreeing with the
    fixed ramp.
  * The fast-stage threshold was measured, not guessed: 4 → c432 281 assemblies (its stages
    mostly converge in 5), 6 → 245, 8 → 228; the chains solved at every value.
  * **Answers:** `deck-diff --all` against 1.23.0, 68 of 72 identical; the four that move are
    the rescue's decks — c432 (140 of 15 416 values, the largest a 3.6e-14 A branch current in
    its last digit, no node voltage), `bsimsoi`/`lutsoi` (≤ 9.8e-15 of scale), c17's transient
    (13 487 → 13 462 steps, node voltages RMS ≤ 4.4e-5 of scale, edges ≤ 7.5 fs). `hisim2` fails
    as before. `validate` 28/28, errors unchanged.
  * **Speed:** c432 `.op`, 8 threads, mains power, two interleaved rounds: 14.96 / 14.55 s →
    **11.66 / 11.28 s (1.3×)**; 293 → 228 Newton iterations. PSP103 chains (`xtask ladder`):
    109–110 → 54–62 iterations. Over today's releases c432 has gone 80.5 s (1.18.0, 1 thread;
    87.5 s at 1.16.0) → 11.5 s on 8 threads.

**Limitations:** tuned on five decks of one technology; the back-off path is exercised by a
scripted test, not by any deck (none failed a stage); a stage failing for a reason a smaller step
cannot fix is retried three times first.

Measured at this version: `cargo test --workspace` 905 passed (+2); `validate` 28/28, errors
unchanged; clippy clean.

1.23.0 - 2026-09-26

**The DC rescue tries the node-step-capped `gmin` ladder before the plain one**
(`docs/proposals/dc-rescue.md`). New tier order after a failed plain solve: ladder + cap →
ladder → ladder + damping. On every circuit measured that needs the rescue the capped ladder is
never more expensive, and where the plain ladder fails it fails slowly — c432 spent 265 Newton
iterations in it before the capped ladder converged. The plain ladder stays as the next tier, so
a circuit the cap cannot solve and the ladder can is still solved (unit-tested).

  * **Answers:** `deck-diff --all` against 1.21.0, 71 of 72 identical — the four NAND sweeps
    whose failing points the plain ladder rescued reach the same printed digits, `hisim2` fails
    as before, c432 takes the same path. c17's transient moves (its starting point was rescued by
    the plain ladder): 13 450 → 13 487 steps, node voltages resampled RMS ≤ 5.9e-5 of scale,
    edges ≤ 12 fs. PSP103 chains of 40/80 stages (not under `circuits/`): ≤ 1.6e-16 V.
  * The "earlier tiers keep their bits" property the old order was built for now protects plain
    Newton alone — where almost every circuit is solved.
  * **Limitation, now wider:** the cap is 0.5 of a node's own unit; on a thermal or optical node
    that may slow a rescue that the plain ladder would have done faster. The plain ladder is
    reached only if the capped one fails, not if it is slow.

**Gate and speed:** the real build is identical on all 72 decks to the scratch build the
proposal measured (`deck-diff --all`), so its 1.21.0 comparison above carries over. c432 `.op`,
8 threads, mains power, two interleaved rounds: **28.3 / 29.3 s → 15.2 / 15.4 s (1.9×)**, from
558 Newton iterations to 293. Since 1.18.0 that deck has gone 80.5 s → 15.3 s.

unchanged; clippy clean.

1.21.0 - 2026-09-26
-------------------
**DC solves use a block-triangular (BTF) solver before `faer`'s sparse LU — Step 3 of
`docs/proposals/btf-solver.md`.** A DC logic Jacobian is almost block triangular by value, so
`va_core::btf` permutes it into its diagonal blocks (maximum transversal + Tarjan SCC), factors
each densely with partial pivoting, and substitutes in block order. The blocks come from the
**running union** of every entry that has been nonzero in a solve so far, and are recomputed only
when the union grows (measured: c432's union is fixed after the second Newton iteration, though
its nonzero set changes on almost every other one). The system goes to `faer` instead — for that
solve only, with no change in what "failed" means — when the union has no full transversal, a
block exceeds `MAX_BLOCK` (64), a block is numerically singular, or the block answer fails the
residual check every sparse answer passes.

  * `SparseLu::with_btf()` and `BtfStats`; `NewtonConfig::btf` (default on) — every DC Newton
    solve on the sparse path, i.e. above 100 unknowns: `.op`, `.dc` sweeps, and the operating
    point that starts a transient, AC or noise run. The transient integrator's own per-step solves and
    AC keep `faer` alone (their matrices keep one large block).
  * `VA_BTF=off` selects `faer` alone. `--logfull` reports `btf=` per iteration and
    `btf_solves=`/`btf_fallbacks=` per stage (`docs/validation.md`).

**Gates:** `deck-diff --all` against 1.18.0 (the last release that changed a number):
with `VA_BTF=off`, 72 of 72 identical — the `faer` path is untouched; with BTF on, 70 of 72
identical. The two that move are the only decks under `circuits/` whose DC solve is sparse:
c432 (329 of 15 416 printed values; one 4.1e-13 A branch current in its 7th digit, the rest
numerical zeros, no node voltage) and c17's transient, whose starting operating point moves in
its last digits and so its step sequence (13 538 → 13 450 points; node voltages resampled RMS
≤ 6.6e-5 of scale, edges ≤ 12 fs). `validate` 28/28, errors unchanged (no golden deck reaches
the sparse path). New unit tests: agreement with `faer`, a pivot inside a block, the same
`Singular` as `faer`, a too-large block giving `faer`'s bits exactly, the union re-analysing
only when it grows, and a 150-stage feed-forward Newton solve agreeing with BTF on and off —
with the block path shown to answer its system.

**Speed,** 8 threads, mains power, two interleaved rounds. c432 `.op`: the block path answered
557 of 558 solves (one handed to `faer`); total solve **22.7 s → 1.20 s** (`--logfull`);
wall **45.5 / 44.6 s → 28.0 / 27.9 s (1.6×)**. chain160 `.op`: 4.36 / 4.30 → 2.87 / 2.79 s
(1.5×). The proposal estimated ~22 s for c432; what is left is almost all device evaluation
(558 Newton iterations over 38 gmin stages — option (C) of the proposal is next).

**Limitations:** serial; a block over `MAX_BLOCK` sends the whole matrix to `faer`, not that
block; the answer moves in the last digits against `faer` (accepted, 2026-09-26).

Measured at this version: `cargo test --workspace` 903 passed (+6 `btf`); `validate` 28/28,
convergence 28/28; clippy clean.

`docs/proposals/btf-solver.md`, measured (§6.1). Verdict: go.** No simulator change.

1.19.1 - 2026-09-26
-------------------
**Proposal: a block-triangular (BTF) front end for the sparse DC solve**
(`docs/proposals/btf-solver.md`). No simulator change. At 8 threads the linear solve is about
half of a large DC run (c432 22.7 of 43.6 s; chain160 1.62 of 3.55 s) and, being serial, does
not shrink with cores. By value, a DC logic matrix is almost triangular: c432 splits into
10 829 blocks, the largest 52 unknowns, whose dense factorization is 1.6 × 10⁶ flops (~1 ms)
against `faer`'s 40 ms per solve. Proposed: (A) BTF on the nonzero pattern, small blocks dense,
large ones through `faer` — measured in `xtask` before anything enters `va-core`. The BTF itself
costs 7 ms on c432, so it must be cached; how often the nonzero set changes during a Newton
solve is the first measurement. Expected (not measured): ~20 s off c432 at 8 threads.
Decided: a last-digit shift in DC answers is acceptable for this.

`cargo xtask btf` now also prints, per pattern, the unknowns in blocks larger than one, the
dense-block LU work Σ s³/3, and how long the BTF itself took. `va-core`'s `sparse.rs` header
said the sparse path was "not wired into any analysis yet" — untrue since the sparse-solve
steps landed; it now says which analyses use it and why, and points at the BTF proposal.

Measured at this version: `cargo test --workspace` 896 passed; `validate` 28/28, convergence
28/28; clippy clean.


1.19.0 - 2026-09-26
-------------------
**`cargo xtask btf <deck> [--model <m>] [--time-lu]`: how far a circuit's Jacobian splits into
block triangular form** — Step 4's first measurement (`docs/proposals/parallel-assembly.md`
§5.1.1). It assembles the deck at its DC operating point, takes a maximum transversal and the
strongly connected components of the permuted graph, and reports blocks, sizes and dependency
depth for the stored pattern and for the numerically nonzero one (DC `G`; transient `G` or `C`),
each also as a union over four bias points. `--time-lu` times `faer`'s LU with and without the
exact zeros. New public `va_cli::instances` (a deck's instances and dimension, for tooling).

What it found, c432 (15 416 unknowns): the stored pattern is one block (99.7%); the DC nonzero
pattern is **10 829 blocks, the largest 52 unknowns**, 72 deep; transient keeps one block of
43%. chain160: 4 024 blocks of ≤ 9, 324 deep; transient 39%. Explicit zeros are *not* the LU's
cost (c432 34 → 31 ms per solve without them). At 8 threads the linear solve is now about half
of each run: c432 22.7 s of 43.6 s (558 iterations × 40.7 ms), chain160 1.62 s of 3.55 s — the
next lever, and the subject of the BTF-solver decision document.

Also fixes 1.18.0's `Cargo.lock`, committed with the crates still at 1.17.0 (the version was
bumped after the last build); `cargo build --locked` would have refused it.

**Limitations:** four bias points are evidence, not proof, that an entry is zero everywhere; the
DAG depth counts blocks, not work.

Measured at this version: `cargo test --workspace` 896 passed (893 + 3 for `btf`); `validate`
28/28, convergence 28/28; clippy clean.

**Device instances are evaluated in parallel, with results bit-identical to serial evaluation
at every thread count.** Step 3 of `docs/proposals/parallel-assembly.md`. `va_core::par::load_all`
evaluates each instance into its own stamp recording on rayon's pool, then replays the
recordings into the system in instance order, so the system receives exactly the serial loop's
calls in the serial loop's order. Used by every DC assembly (dense and sparse) and every
transient assembly; AC and noise load once per analysis and stay serial.

  * **§6 interface change, ratified 2026-09-26:** `ModelInstance: Send + Sync`
    (`docs/interfaces.md`). `va-codegen`: shared model and gradient buffers `Rc` → `Arc`, lazy
    setup `RefCell<Option<_>>` → `OnceLock<_>`; one `va-transient` test double `Cell` →
    `AtomicUsize`. Per-evaluation scratch is untouched.
  * **When it goes parallel is measured, not a count:** thread hand-off costs ~40 µs per
    assembly here, which made the 13-instance reference-model `ring_osc` 2.2× *slower* in
    parallel (0.54 vs 0.24 s) while c17's ~30 PSP103 transistors ran 2.0× *faster* (82 vs
    165 s, 8 threads, two runs each). So `load_all` times a serial assembly now and then
    (first call, then every 256th), keeps an evaluation-time-per-instance estimate, and goes
    parallel when `instances × estimate` ≥ 200 µs (`PARALLEL_MIN_WORK_NS`). The choice
    depends on timing; the result does not.
  * `va-cli sim --threads <n>` sizes the pool (default: one per logical core, or
    `RAYON_NUM_THREADS`). `VA_PARALLEL=auto|always|never` forces the path, for verification.
  * `cargo xtask deck-diff` gained `--env-old K=V` / `--env-new K=V`, to compare one binary
    with itself under two environments — the gate below.

**Gate:** `deck-diff --all`, 72 of 72 decks identical in each of: 1.17.0 vs 1.18.0 (defaults);
1 thread vs `VA_PARALLEL=always` at 2, 4 and 8 threads. deck-diff compares printed output; the

calls as direct stamping, for all six methods, and `load_all` in pools of 1/2/4/8 threads

**Speed,** c432 `.op` (ISCAS'85: 2 458 devices, 910 of them PSP103; 15 416 unknowns), this

**Limitations:** replay is serial and a recording is allocated per instance per assembly; the

**Dual-number operations write their result into an operand's gradient buffer when nothing else
holds it: PSP103 `load()` a further 23% faster, 42% faster than 1.14.0.** `zip_with` is now one
implementation over owned gradients with three cases — both zero (no allocation), exactly one zero

variable's value (shared with the variable table) is never overwritten.

not reduced to two coefficients `ca·p + cb·q`, which rounds differently for `div` — so every
result is **bit-identical** and the change could be checked against every deck exactly.

1.14.0+1 - 2026-09-24
---------------------
**Dual-number gradients live in one shared allocation (`Rc<[f64]>`): copying a value no longer
copies its gradient, and a new gradient is one heap allocation. PSP103 `load()` is 24% faster.**
Reading a local variable copies its value; 1.14.0's counters showed those copies were 47% of every
gradient vector a PSP103 evaluation builds. A gradient is never modified after it is built, so
sharing it changes no arithmetic: every result is bit-identical.

Measured, `xtask bench-model` on PSP103's `load()`, seven runs of each binary alternated: median
172.7 → 130.2 µs, best 157.4 → 120.0 µs; the two sets do not overlap. Heap allocations per `load()`
(a counting allocator, scratch build): **1 845 → 988**.

**How it was found, because the first attempt measured nothing.** It was first written as
`Rc<Vec<f64>>` and showed no speed-up (216 → 221 µs), which I read as "allocation is not the cost"
and wrote into the docs. A sampling profile (samply, attached to `bench-model`) said otherwise:
~29% of the run inside the heap allocator, called from `Dual::mul/add/sub`, plus freeing
(`Rc::drop_slow` 10.5%). The cause was the first version itself: `Rc<Vec<f64>>` takes **two**
allocations per new gradient (the `Rc` box and the buffer), so removing 874 copies added ~970
allocations — 1 845 → 1 946 per `load()`, a net increase. `grad_allocs` counts gradient vectors,
not heap allocations, and could not see it. `Rc<[f64]>`, collected straight from the iterator, is
one. The profile and its analysis script are kept locally (`/perf-logs/`).

Still open, from the same profile: the tree walk itself (`eval` ~19% self time, `get_var`/
`set_var` ~6%) and the remaining ~990 allocations per `load()` — about 22% of which are
one-zero-operand results in `zip_with`, a full-length scaled copy that operators consuming their
operands could write in place.

Measured at 1.14.0+1: `cargo test --workspace` **887 passed / 0 failed** (the finite-difference
Jacobian checks included). fmt + clippy clean. `cargo xtask validate`: **28/28**, identical to

**The netlist parser reads SPICE deck structure — `.subckt`, `.model` cards, `+` continuations,

  * `.subckt`/`.ends` are flattened, with ngspice's naming: internal net `sig3` of instance `X5`

declarations **ignoring case** (`Device::spice_names`): a card's `psp103va` and `l=` reach

refused. Plain `X` lines keep Verilog-A's exact matching.

It was not one: twelve lines with no compact model reproduce it.

`va_codegen::lower::ideal_shorts` proves, per compiled model and under its parameter values,
which branches are ideal shorts. Only what can be proven counts: every potential contribution
that can run folds to zero from literals and parameters, no flow contribution can run (for
BSIM-BULK's mixed `if (SHMOD...) Pwr(t) <+ ... else Temp(t) <+ 0.0`, the condition folds from
parameters), and the branch current is never read. `va-cli` runs a union-find over every short
in deck order (`ShortForest`). A short whose ends are already held equal is pinned to zero
current through the new `CompiledModel::instantiate_with_shorts`. That covers duplicates,
reversed duplicates, a short from a node to itself, and loops of shorts. Node voltages and
every other current are unchanged. The pinned instance reports `0` on its own auxiliary row
(`X2.b0`) and the kept one reports the total, which is the one visible difference.

Limitations: a mixed branch whose arm depends on a variable, even one computed from parameters
alone, is not proven to be a short and is left alone. Loops through deck voltage sources or a
nonzero `V(p,n) <+ expr` are still singular, as in any MNA simulator.

a loop of shorts, and which branches qualify); with pinning disabled the two deck tests fail
with the original `singular matrix`. `cargo xtask validate` **28 checked, 0 failed, 28/28**,

1.3.2 - 2026-09-22
------------------
**The elaborated IR is shared across instances of the same model.** `build_instance` cloned the
whole `va_ir::Module` per device and lowered it again, so a deck paid for the arena once per
*device* rather than once per *model card*. For PSP103 that arena is large: a chain of 40
CMOS inverters (80 devices, two cards) peaked at **413 MB** and took **14.1 s**; the same deck
now peaks at **78 MB** in **9.1 s**. Per device the memory slope falls from 5.1 MB to 0.78 MB,
and what is left is the dense Jacobian, not the IR:

    devices     10      20      40      80
    before    56.5   103.4   199.4   413.2  MB
    after     23.3    25.5    36.4    78.1  MB

`va_codegen::GeneratedModel` is now an `Rc<SharedModel>` (module, resolved parameters, lowered
plan, junction classification, evaluated setup) plus the one thing that is genuinely per
instance: the local-slot -> global-unknown map. The new `CompiledModel::{new, instantiate}`
exposes the split; `build_instance` is unchanged and now calls both, so nothing outside
`va-cli` had to move. `va-ir` and `va-abi` are untouched — this is not an Interface alpha/beta
change.

**What the sharing is keyed on, and why that is the whole correctness question.** `va-cli`
caches compiled models per `(module, positional value, named overrides, unconnected ports)`.
The first three are obvious in hindsight — parameter values are baked into the compiled model,
so a PMOS written `TYPE=-1` is not the NMOS written `TYPE=1`. The fourth was not: marking a
port unconnected *mutates* the module, and `$port_connected` changes what the elaborated code
does, so two devices differing only in whether they wire an optional terminal need two
compiled models. That analysis therefore had to move ahead of the compile.

Each of the three keyed properties was checked by deliberately dropping it and confirming a
gate turns red, not by assuming: `two_devices_from_one_model_keep_their_own_parameters` (new)
reports 0.5 V instead of 0.75 V when either parameter half is dropped, and the existing
`fiber_mzi_noise_matches_wanser_and_duan_through_the_interferometer` fails when port
connectivity is dropped — `circuits/fiber_mzi_noise.net` places two waveguides with identical
overrides, one with its PZT control port wired and one without.

The cost, stated: models with *different* parameters still compile separately, which is
correct rather than optimised away, so a deck of N individually-tuned devices sees none of
this. The sweep rebuild path also keeps a per-point cache, since the swept value is part of
the key and nothing could be shared across points anyway.

Measured at this version: `cargo test --workspace` **839 passed / 0 failed** (838 + the new
key test); `cargo xtask validate` **28 checked, 0 failed, convergence 28/28**; corpus
`check external --recursive` 69/73 self-contained, 86 passes — all unchanged. `cargo fmt` and
`cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings` clean.

1.3.1 - 2026-09-22
------------------
**`gmin` stepping is wired in — as a rescue, not as a routine.** `va_core::convergence` has had
the homotopy since T3.3 and `NewtonConfig::gmin_steps` defaulted to **0**, so it never ran. A
node with no DC path was therefore simply fatal, and that is the ordinary shape of a series
stack: in a CMOS NAND the node between the two pull-down devices floats whenever the lower one
is off. Six of the twelve decks in `circuits/benchmark/nand/` could not be solved at all.

  **Eleven of twelve now solve; eight are clean NANDs.** BSIM-CMG and L-UTSOI became clean
  (1.200000 V -> 3.70e-8 V and 1.41e-10 V); BSIM-SOI and EKV2.6 now solve but pull down only to
  0.437 V and 1.099 V, which at default parameters is the device rather than the solver. HiSIM2
  still fails for its own reasons, and BSIM-IMG's deck is wrong rather than its solve.

  **The plain solve is tried first, always.** Only a failure the homotopy can plausibly rescue
  — `Singular`, `NoConvergence` or `NonFinite`, each observed on a real deck — earns a second
  attempt with the ladder, and a caller that already asked for stepping is left alone. That
  ordering is the design, not an optimisation: the ladder is `gmin_steps + 1` full Newton
  solves, so running it unconditionally would multiply the cost of every DC point in every
  circuit, including the large majority that converge on the first try and need none of it.
  As a fallback those circuits pay **nothing** and their answers stay bit-identical.

  The evidence for that last claim is the gate: `cargo xtask validate` is 28/28 with all 28
  error figures **identical digit for digit** to the run before the change. A test states it
  directly too, comparing a converging circuit's solution through `dc::operating_point` against
  `newton::solve` with `==` rather than a tolerance.

  **Both DC entry points share one rescue.** `dc::operating_point` gets it as well as
  `operating_point_continued`, because `.ac` and `.noise` linearize around the former — without
  that, a circuit with a floating node would solve under `.op`/`.dc` and fail under `.ac`, the
  same circuit with a different verdict.

  **The ladder's size was measured, not chosen.** 30 stages at >=150 iterations each: 10, 20, 30
  and 40 stages all still failed the 20-diode chain at the default 100-iteration budget, so the
  binding constraint was the per-stage budget rather than the ramp. The final unshunted stage
  has to walk from the last shunted solution to the real answer, and on a long series chain that
  is more than 100 steps. Only failed solves ever pay for it.

  **If the rescue also fails, the *original* error surfaces.** It describes the real circuit and
  names the row that went singular or non-finite; the laddered one describes a shunted variant
  the user never wrote.

  Measured at this version. `cargo test --workspace` 838 passed / 0 failed (2 new). `cargo xtask
  validate` 28 checked, 0 failed golden, convergence 28/28, every figure identical. Corpus
  `va-cli check external --codegen` 200/224.

  **Not fixed, and still the next thing in that folder:** two instances of a five-port model
  sharing one thermal node give a singular matrix, reproduced in four lines in
  `circuits/benchmark/nand/README.md` §2. `gmin` does not rescue it — it shunts `Node` rows, and
  whatever is degenerate there is not one. Root cause still not established.

1.3.0 - 2026-09-22
------------------
**`$table_model` — a user can bring their own data.** LRM §9.21, one dimension, file source.
The first answer to "could we let users add their own `$` functions": the LRM already has one,
and it covers what people usually mean by it — measured data, a lookup — without opening a hole
in the AD contract that a black-box function would.

    I(p, n) <+ V(p, n) / $table_model(V(t), "thermistor.tbl", "1CL");

  **The file is read at elaboration, not at evaluation**, and that is the load-bearing choice.
  It is what the LRM describes (§9.21.1: the data source's state "is captured on the first
  call") and what keeps `ModelInstance::load` a pure function of `(x, ctx, committed state)`. A
  lookup that touched the filesystem per evaluation would be re-run by every Newton iteration
  and by every rejected timestep, and would break both. Only the numbers reach the IR — no path,
  no handle, no reader — so Interface β is untouched.

  The table rides **flattened into the call's own arguments**, the same trade `noise_table` made
  in 2026-08-04: an `Expr` variant carrying a `Vec<(f64, f64)>` would have to be taught to every
  arena walk, clone, const-folder and pretty-printer, while `Const` arguments to an ordinary
  `Call` are already handled by all of them. Interface α change, recorded in
  `docs/interfaces.md` with the argument layout and the packed control code.

  **The derivative is the point.** A lookup whose Jacobian did not match its value would not
  error — it would converge Newton to the wrong answer, the failure mode three separate bugs
  took this week. So the segment slope is returned with the value and finite-difference gated
  per §5 (`table_model_derivative_matches_finite_difference`). At a knot the function has a
  kink and no derivative exists; the right-hand segment is taken, the same subgradient
  convention `abs` uses at zero, and that is written down rather than left to be discovered.

  **Defaults are the LRM's, which are not the obvious ones:** linear interpolation and *linear*
  extrapolation at both ends, not constant. A model that wants its table clamped has to say so.

  **Refused, not approximated** — each a `Refusal` naming what to write instead: a second lookup
  expression (N-D), the array data source, `2`/`3` spline interpolation, `I` ignore-column, `E`
  error-extrapolation, and the `;N` dependent-column selector. A missing or malformed file stays
  a plain elaboration error, because that is a broken input rather than a declined construct, and
  §5 makes the distinction the first thing a user debugging a run needs. Notably `E` cannot be
  honoured at all: it makes an out-of-range lookup a fatal error, and `load` may not fail.

  **Template example**, the second half of the request: `models/thermistor.va` +
  `models/thermistor.tbl` + `circuits/thermistor_rt.net`. An NTC thermistor whose resistance is
  a measured R(T) table rather than a formula, with the table driven by a *node voltage* so it
  participates in the circuit equations rather than being read once at setup. Verified against
  the table's own rows: exact at every knot, 5630.0000 Ω interpolated at 295.15 K against a
  hand-computed 5630.0000, constant below the range and linear above it. The model's own comment
  works out where `"L"` at the top end reaches zero resistance (≈398 K, 25 K past the data) —
  because a control string is a judgement and the example should say what it costs.

  Also: `elaborate_unit` now takes the compilation unit's include path, so a data file resolves
  exactly as an `` `include `` does. The older entry points pass an empty path and keep working.

  Measured at this version. `cargo test --workspace` 836 passed / 0 failed (4 new AD tests).
  `cargo xtask validate` 28 checked, 0 failed golden, convergence 28/28. Corpus `va-cli check
  external --codegen` 200/224, 182/191 self-contained.

  **On the version.** Published first as `1.2.6` and relabelled **1.3.0** the same day: §12 has a
  feature bump the minor after 1.0, and `$table_model` is a feature rather than one of the 1.2

1.2.5 - 2026-09-22
------------------
**A capacitor pinned by a fast-slewing source could not be integrated at all.** The step
controller rejected every step down to the floor and gave up:

    V1 in gnd PULSE(0 1.2 0p 200p 100p 1n 2n)
    C1 in gnd 1.7f
    R1 in out 10k
    .tran 10p 1n          ->  timestep underflow at t=0

That deck is now `circuits/benchmark/cap_fast_edge.net`, and it contains **no Verilog-A** —
reference capacitor, resistor and waveform source. The bug was never about compact models; it
only looked that way, because a CMOS gate input is exactly this circuit, and it is what stopped
a PSP103 inverter from switching.

  **Cause.** A transient starts from the operating point, which carries no reactive current by
  definition, so on the first step the source's branch current jumps from zero to `C·dV/dt`.
  `divided_difference_error_ratio` error-controls *every* unknown including that row, with a
  formula that assumes smoothness — and a discontinuity's divided difference does not shrink
  with `h`. Shrinking is a remedy for truncation error, not for a discontinuity. Confirmed to be
  the LTE path and not Newton by instrumenting both, and the threshold matched `lte_abstol`
  (1e-6 A) exactly: at 6e9 V/s, `C = 1e-16` passed and `C = 2e-16` failed.

  **Fix.** A row that carries no integrated state — `Branch` by
  `va_core::mna::classify_unknowns`, not dynamic by `classify_dynamic_rows`, in practice a
  voltage source's branch current — loses its veto **at the floor**, the last step size the
  controller would try. An inductor's branch row is also `Branch` and keeps its veto: it carries
  flux, so it is dynamic, and its current is continuous precisely because it is integrated.

  **Two wrong versions came first, and both are the reason the final one looks the way it
  does.** Recording them because the shape of this fix is not guessable from the bug:

  * *Excluding those rows outright* fixed the capacitor and broke other things. Rows without
    integrated state still say something useful about how fast a circuit is moving:
    `motorway_ramp_mpc` underflowed at t = 3600 s where it had run, because the step grew past
    what Newton could take across the metering step change, and `circuits/rectifier.net` lost
    23% of its timepoints (718 -> 551) and 19% of its accuracy against golden (8.269e-4 ->
    1.027e-3, past its own 1e-3 tolerance). A structural rule cannot tell "discontinuous" from
    "merely sharp", and both look identical in the estimate.
  * *Relaxing for one step only* hung. The controller accepts at the floor, grows the step,
    meets the same discontinuity, shrinks back and accepts again — about 1e8 steps for a 1 ns
    window. A hang is worse than the error it replaced. The relaxation therefore **latches**:
    once a run has proved that shrinking is futile, those rows stay out for the rest of it.

  So the only code path that changes is the one that previously returned `TimestepUnderflow`.
  Anything converging at a larger step is untouched, which is what makes the claim below
  possible.

  validate` 28 checked, 0 failed golden, convergence 28/28 — **every figure identical, digit for
  digit**, to the run before this change. Corpus `va-cli check external --codegen` 200/224.
  `circuits/benchmark/cap_fast_edge.net` reaches tstop in 120 timepoints with `V(out)` exactly

**A transient now starts from the DC operating point, as SPICE does, unless the deck says
`UIC`.** It used to start from the zero vector plus each element's `IC=`, unconditionally, with
no way to ask for anything else — and `cargo xtask gen-golden` carried a matching workaround
that forced `UIC` onto every deck it handed QSPICE, because the two had to be made to agree
somehow.

  **This was not a stylistic difference. It made every CMC MOSFET unrunnable in transient.** At
  `x = 0` a compact model's charge is not consistent with any solution, so the first step has to
  move all of it — and halving the timestep makes the required current *larger*, not smaller, so
  the step controller can only shrink until it underflows. BSIM4, BSIM-BULK 107, BSIM-SOI,
  PSP103 and EKV2.6 all failed at `t = 0` on every release before this one, and none of them
  could ever have run. All five integrate now. Verified against v1.1.1 that this is pre-existing
  and not a regression from this week's work.

  Why nothing caught it: the zoo's transient gates are RC, RLC and diode circuits whose zero
  start *is* physically consistent (a capacitor at 0 V holds no charge), and the zoo's own
  MOSFET model carries no charge at all.

  `.tran <tstep> <tstop> [UIC]` is parsed by `va-netlist`; `Netlist::tran_uic` carries it.

  **No goldens were regenerated, and none needed to be.** Flipping the default broke six gates —
  `rc_step`, `rc_discharge`, `rlc_ring`, `rl_decay`, `ring_osc`, `laplace_step` — and those six

  an operating point are flat lines. They meant `UIC` all along; their `.tran` cards now say so,

  generating those goldens with QSPICE's `UIC` forced on. `rc_step_hv`, `rectifier`,
  `abstime_ramp` and `vsin_load` were unaffected either way: their operating point at `t = 0`

      V1 in gnd PULSE(0 1.2 0p 200p 100p 1n 2n)
      C1 in gnd 1.7f
      R1 in out 10k
      .tran 10p 1n          -> timestep underflow at t=0

  A capacitor whose voltage is pinned by a source slewing fast at `t = 0` gives the source's
  branch current a step discontinuity across the first step — the operating point has no
  reactive current by construction, the first transient point has `C·dV/dt`.
  `divided_difference_error_ratio` estimates the error over **every** unknown, including that
  branch-current row, and a discontinuity's divided difference does not shrink with `h`, so
  every step is rejected down to underflow. Confirmed to be the LTE path and not Newton by
  instrumenting both. The threshold is quantitative and matches exactly: failure begins when
  `C·dV/dt` crosses `lte_abstol` (1e-6 A) — at 6e9 V/s, `C = 1e-16` passes and `C = 2e-16`
  fails. The fix belongs in `va-transient`: error-control the reactive states rather than every
  row, and do not LTE-control a step across a known discontinuity.

**`@(above(...))` in a `.dc` sweep fired at every point past its threshold instead of once, on
the crossing.** LRM §5.10.2: `above(expr)` triggers when `expr` *becomes* positive. A standalone
operating point has no earlier point to have been non-positive at, so there "already positive"
is the trigger — and `va_core::dc::operating_point_with_events` has taken the previous point's
site values as a `previous` argument since the event channel was built, for exactly the sweep
case. `solve_dc_sweep` never threaded them and passed `None` at every point, so a signal that
went past its threshold at the third point re-fired at the fourth, fifth and every point after.
A latch that never latched.

  Sweeping `V1` 0 -> 1 V through `@(above(V(p,n) - 0.5)) g = 1.0;` over a `g = 1e-3` default:

      V1        before        after
      0.0-0.5   1 mS          1 mS
      0.6       1 S           1 S      <- the crossing
      0.7-1.0   1 S           1 mS     <- did not *become* positive here

  The history is not reset when a warm start fails and the point is retried cold (v1.2.2's
  fallback): which sites crossed is a property of the two operating points, not of the path
  Newton took to the second one.

  **This changes results**, which is the point of it — it is the first change in this series
  that does. Any deck with an `above` in a swept analysis gets different numbers, and they are
  the LRM's numbers. `cargo xtask validate` is 28/28 with all figures unchanged, because no zoo
  deck puts an `above` in a `.dc` sweep — which is also why nothing caught this.

  `above_fires_once_on_the_crossing_of_a_dc_sweep_not_at_every_point_after` is the bug itself;

  `above_fires_at_the_first_point_of_a_sweep_that_starts_past_the_threshold` is its companion

  initialization case and must still fire. Implementing this as "never fire without a previous

**A `.dc` sweep continues from the previous point instead of restarting Newton at the origin.**
Adjacent points of a sweep are the same circuit at a slightly different source value, so the
answer at one point is an excellent guess for the next — a couple of iterations instead of a
dozen from zero. Every point had been solved cold, which is the one thing every other SPICE
does not do.

    circuit                          v1.2.1    v1.2.2
    BSIM4 Id-Vg, 1001 points          3434 ms    626 ms    5.5x
    HICUM/L2v3 output family,         2811 ms   1050 ms    2.7x
      6 x 201 = 1206 points                                (4.1x net of six process launches)

Both best of three: this machine's run-to-run spread on these is 20-40%, and a single
measurement of either would have been worth little.

  New in `va-core`, both additive — the old entry points are one-line delegations and behave
  exactly as before: `newton::solve_with_events_from` and `dc::operating_point_continued` take
  an optional starting point. A `start` whose width is not `dim` is refused rather than padded
  or truncated, since either would silently solve a different problem.

  **On failure the point is retried cold.** Continuation is an accelerator, not a change of
  contract: a guess can in principle be worse than the origin, and a sweep that used to produce
  an answer must not stop producing one because the solver was handed a hint. With the retry the
  set of points that converge can only grow. Stated plainly: **that fallback is not exercised by
  a test**, because constructing a circuit where a warm start fails and a cold start succeeds is
  a research question rather than a fixture. What is tested is that it cannot make things worse
  by construction.

  **It moves no answers.** All 1001 BSIM4 sweep points are identical to the run taken before any
  of this week's work, digit for digit; so are all 1206 HICUM points and all 28 figures in
  `cargo xtask validate`, three of which are `.dc` sweeps. Stronger than the printed digits:
  `a_reused_instance_set_sweeps_exactly_as_per_point_rebuilds_would` compares raw solution
  vectors with `==` against a cold, per-point-rebuilt solve and passes, so on that circuit
  continuation moves not a single bit. That test now covers both sweep optimisations at once.

  `a_starting_point_changes_the_path_not_the_answer` checks a nonlinear circuit from four
  starting points including the origin — and, because that would pass just as well if `start`
  were quietly ignored, also checks that with a **one-iteration budget** a solve starting at the
  answer converges while one starting at the origin fails. That second half is what makes it
  discriminating.

  Measured at this version. `cargo test --workspace` 825 passed / 0 failed (4 new: 2 in
  `va-core`, and the widened `va-cli` pair from v1.2.1). `cargo xtask validate` 28 checked,
  0 failed golden, convergence 28/28, all figures identical to v1.2.1's run. Corpus `va-cli
  check external --codegen` 200/224, 182/191 self-contained.

  **Not done, deliberately.** `solve_dc_sweep` still passes `None` for `AboveValues` at every

1.2.1 - 2026-09-22

**A `.dc` sweep builds its instances once instead of once per point.** `solve_dc_sweep` cloned
the deck at every swept value and called `solve_dc`, which rebuilt every device in it —
including each compiled Verilog-A model. `va_codegen::build_instance` is 7.3 ms for BSIM4
against 10.7 ms for a whole sweep point, so roughly two thirds of a sweep was reconstructing
models that had not changed, and it threw away each model's cached setup (v1.2.0's
`Lowered::static_prefix`) every point, turning once-per-instance work into once-per-point work.

Now the deck is built once and only the swept source is rebuilt in place — through the same
`build_instance` call the original construction used, not a copy of it, with the global unknown
counter rewound to what it held before that device first claimed anything, so the replacement
occupies exactly the rows the original did. `BuiltInstances` gained a `slots: Vec<DeviceSlot>`
recording each phase-1 device's index and its before/after counter values; it is now a named
struct rather than a four-tuple, which is what makes that fifth field readable at its seven
call sites.

  A rebuild that claimed a different number of unknowns would push every later device's rows
  out of alignment and the solver would quietly solve a different circuit. It cannot happen for
  a source — a voltage source's row count does not depend on its value — so the code says so
  with a real error rather than a `debug_assert`, and the message names the device.

  **Measured, 1001-point BSIM4 Id-Vg sweep: 10.7 s -> 3.17 s**, and every one of the 1001 output
  lines is identical to the run taken before any of v1.2.0's work. End to end against where this
  started, the same sweep was **30.0 s** at v1.1.1: 9.5x, output unchanged throughout.

  Nothing is carried between points. The solve starts from the same initial guess it always did,
  and the only mutable state an instance holds is the cached setup, which is bias-independent by
  construction. Two new tests state that rather than trusting it:
  `a_reused_instance_set_sweeps_exactly_as_per_point_rebuilds_would` compares the whole solution
  vector with `==`, not a tolerance, against a per-point rebuild at every point of the diode
  sweep — a sweep that reused a stale operating point would still converge and still look
  plausible, so an approximate comparison is the wrong instrument. It was confirmed to
  discriminate by dropping the in-place replacement and watching it fail at the second point.
  `re_pointing_the_swept_source_does_not_claim_new_unknowns` checks the solution vector never
  widens and that the source is still actually driving its node.

  Measured at this version. `cargo test --workspace` 823 passed / 0 failed (2 new).
  `cargo xtask validate` 28 checked, 0 failed golden, convergence 28/28 — diffed line by line
  against v1.2.0's run, all 28 error figures identical, and that gate contains three `.dc`
  sweeps (`diode_iv`, `diode_clamp`, `diode_iv_params`), which is the part of it this change can
  reach. Corpus `va-cli check external --codegen` 200/224, 182/191 self-contained.

  **Not fixed here, same shape.** `quantities()`, `branch_currents()` and `sizing()` each call
  `build_instances` on their own, so a single `va-cli sim` run still builds the deck three or
  four times before it solves anything — a fixed cost, not a per-point one, but it is three
  wasted `build_instance` calls per compiled model and it is why a one-point `.op` on BSIM4
  still costs ~48 ms of which ~16 ms is the frontend. And `.tran`/`.ac`/`.noise` build once
  already, so they were never affected by this.

**The evaluator stops paying for work it does not do.** One `ModelInstance::load()` — the
residual + Jacobian evaluation every Newton iteration of every timepoint pays for — is 3.4x to
9.3x cheaper on the CMC standard models, with every golden number bit-identical. Measured
before and after with the same new tool, `cargo xtask bench-model`:

      model            exprs  unkns   stmts   setup    before     after   speedup
      BSIM4            19109     18    1949    1556   1215 us    131 us      9.3x
      PSP103           40807     21    1220     757    987 us    161 us      6.1x
      BSIM-SOI         16301     23    1216     700   1045 us    159 us      6.6x
      BSIM-BULK107     19070     24     932     282   1067 us    212 us      5.0x
      HICUM/L2v3        7408     22     191       1    271 us     61 us      4.4x
      JUNCAP200        12932      3     215     194    200 us     49 us      4.1x
      EKV2.6            2490      4     268      47    278 us     81 us      3.4x

End to end, a 1001-point BSIM4 Id-Vg sweep went from 30.0 s to 10.7 s, output unchanged to the
last digit.

Three changes, in descending order of what they bought:

  * **`Dual`'s gradient knows how to be nothing.** It was two `Vec<f64>` of length `n_unknowns`,
    allocated for every intermediate value — including the 78-87% of them that depend on no
    unknown at all (7816 of BSIM4's 9020 per `load`, measured). A gradient channel is now
    `Grad::Zero` or `Grad::Dense`, and `Zero` holds no allocation and needs no length. Exact,
    not heuristic: a value is independent of the unknowns precisely when both its operands
    were. ~3.1x on its own.
  * **Local variables are indexed, not hashed.** `Ctx::vars` was a `HashMap<u32, Dual>`; BSIM4
    declares 1446 locals and reads them thousands of times per `load`, paying a SipHash for
    each. Now a `Vec<Option<Dual>>` indexed by `VarId`. A further ~1.6x on BSIM4.
  * **A model is split into setup and eval, and the setup runs once.** Most of a compact model
    is parameter checking, temperature scaling, geometry and binning — bias-independent
    statements that produce the same numbers on iteration 300 as on iteration 1. `lower` now
    computes how many leading statements those are (`Lowered::static_prefix`: 1556 of BSIM4's
    1949, 757 of PSP103's 1220); `load` evaluates them once per instance and starts every later
    call past them. A further ~1.6x on BSIM4, ~1.2x on PSP103, and nothing at all on
    HICUM/L2v3, whose setup is not a prefix (1 statement of 191).

  **Why it is a prefix and not "every bias-independent statement":** nothing is reordered. A
  bias-independent statement sitting after a bias-dependent one may read what that statement
  wrote, so hoisting it needs dependence analysis this does not do. That is the whole of why
  HICUM/L2v3 gains nothing here, and it is the obvious next thing to widen.

  **The classification is a whitelist with no wildcard arm**, so a new `Expr` variant or
  `Builtin` fails to compile until someone decides which side of the split it is on. Getting
  that wrong in the permissive direction would freeze a value at its first evaluation, and the
  solve would simply converge to the wrong answer with nothing to see. `$abstime`, `$simparam`
  (gmin moves under gmin stepping), the analysis-context builtins, every analog operator and
  every noise source are all outside it. `$temperature`/`$vt`/`$mfactor` are inside it because
  they read the temperature the *instance* was built at — **if `$temperature` is ever re-sourced
  from `AnalysisCtx`, the cached setup must be keyed on temperature or it will serve one sweep
  point's numbers to the next.** That caveat is written at `lower::static_prefix_len` too.

  **One behaviour did change, and a test caught it.** A setup that *fails* — a `while` whose
  condition never goes false, hitting the iteration cap — used to abort the walk and skip every
  statement after it. Split into a phase of its own, the failure was being swallowed and the
  contribution downstream of the runaway loop started stamping. The setup now records whether
  it completed and `load` skips the eval phase if it did not, restoring the old behaviour
  (`a_runaway_while_loop_is_bounded_by_the_iteration_cap_not_a_hang`).

  **Corpus 199 -> 200/224, and it is a correctness fix rather than a loosening.**
  `L_UTSOI_102_nqs.va` was being refused for "ddt of a value that already depends on a time
  derivative". It does not contain one. `carries_charge` asked "is any partial non-zero" of a
  dense vector of zeros — and multiplying zeros by an infinite coefficient makes them NaN, with
  `NaN != 0.0` true, so a value carrying no charge reported that it did. Verified on the
  pristine code: excluding NaN from that one predicate alone is enough to make the file pass.
  `Grad::Zero` removes the NaN at the source. The *structural* rule that refuses a genuine
  `ddt(ddt(x))` lives in `lower` and still fires, checked with a minimal model and now with
  `an_infinite_coefficient_does_not_fake_a_charge_channel`. This is the same `0 * inf` family as
  v1.1.1 — the third instance, and the reason the new representation is worth more than its
  speed.

  **New: `cargo xtask bench-model [<model.va>...]`.** `bench-scale` measures the solver on an RC
  ladder of reference primitives, which carry no Verilog-A at all; this measures the other half,
  and on a real compact model the other half is three orders of magnitude larger than the solve
  it stamps into. Defaults to the seven models under `external/code`, skipping any that are
  absent (`external/` is gitignored).

  Measured at this version. `cargo test --workspace` 821 passed / 0 failed (7 new: 5 for the
  setup/eval split, 1 for the charge-channel NaN, plus the discrimination check). `cargo xtask
  validate` 28 checked, 0 failed golden, convergence 28/28 — and **diffed line by line against a
  run of the pristine tree: all 28 error figures are bit-identical**, which is the evidence that
  a 9x change to how the evaluator works moved no answer. Corpus `va-cli check external
  --codegen` 200/224, 182/191 self-contained.

  **What this does not do, and what is next.** It is still a tree-walking interpreter; nothing
  is compiled to machine code. The remaining per-`load` cost is recursive `eval` dispatch,
  `Result` plumbing and arena indirection on the bias-dependent core, which a flat SSA tape
  would remove and a Cranelift JIT (pure Rust, so it clears §5's no-native-link rule, but it
  needs `unsafe` to call what it emits and so needs sign-off under §5) would remove further.
  And the larger fish: **`solve_dc_sweep` rebuilds every model instance at every sweep point**.
  `build_instance` is 7.3 ms for BSIM4 against 10.7 ms per sweep point, so most of a DC sweep is
  now rebuilding a model that did not change — and it throws away the cached setup each time.
  That is a `va-cli` change, not a codegen one, and it is the next thing worth doing.

1.1.1 - 2026-09-21
------------------
**Two AD rules returned NaN where the derivative is finite, and it cost us the big CMC
MOSFETs.** Both are `0 * inf` in `va-codegen`'s dual numbers, and both fire at *x = 0* --
which is not an edge case, it is where every Newton solve starts.

  * `Dual::powf` differentiated the base by the logarithmic rule `u^v * (v*u'/u + v'*ln u)`.
    At `u = 0` that is `0 * inf` = NaN, where `d/du u^1.5 = 1.5*sqrt(u) = 0`; at `u < 0` with
    an integer `v` it routes through `ln(u)` = NaN, where `d/du u^2 = 2u` is finite. It now
    uses the closed form `v*u^(v-1)` for the base and forms the `u^v*ln u` term only when the
    exponent actually varies. `pow(x, k)` and `x*x` now give the same derivative, which is the
    invariant that makes the two spellings interchangeable in a model.
  * `Dual::chain` scaled *every* gradient channel by the outer slope, including channels whose
    incoming gradient is exactly `0.0`. At a function's own singularity the slope is `±inf`,
    so a subexpression built only from parameters -- which cannot affect the Jacobian at all --
    came back with an all-NaN gradient that then propagated into rows it never reached. A zero
    channel now stays zero. A channel the operand *does* depend on still reports the true
    singularity: `d/dx sqrt(x)` at 0 is still `+inf`, because it is.

  Why it took this long to surface: the zoo's own models never raise a bias-dependent quantity
  to a power near zero, and `va-abi`'s reference models are hand-written. `cargo xtask
  validate` was 28/28 before the fix and is 28/28 after it -- the gate could not see this. What
  saw it was `external/code`, the CMC standard-model distributions (BSIM, PSP, HICUM, MEXTRAM,
  EKV, ...), added this release as corpus.

  Measured at this version. `cargo test --workspace` 814 passed / 0 failed (4 new AD tests).
  `cargo xtask validate` 28 checked, 0 failed golden, convergence 28/28. Corpus
  `va-cli check external --codegen` 199/224 files declaring a module, 181/191 self-contained --
  up from 113/132 because `external/code` adds 92 module-declaring files, of which 85 pass
  frontend + codegen.

  What the fix bought, on the seven models the OpenVAF paper benchmarks (each a bare `.op`, no
  model card, so the numbers are the defaults' operating point and not a validated I-V):
  BSIM4, PSP103 and BSIM-BULK 107 went from "NaN Jacobian on iteration 1" to a converged

  Limitations this does not touch. `pow` with `v < 1` at `u = 0` still returns `±inf`, as it

**Every `sim` says what it is about to solve, and roughly what that costs**, before solving
(`va_cli::estimate`, `va_cli::sizing`; the user's request, and `docs/validation.md` now carries
the designer-side reading of the dense-LU limit it is calibrated from):

```
[va-cli] circuit: 11 device(s) (8 compiled), 28 unknown(s) (17 net(s) + 11 auxiliary row(s)), ~12601 points (adaptive, 12601 is the card's floor)
[va-cli] estimate: 0.8-39.1 s of solve, 18.8 kB of matrix -- rough, dense LU scaled from bench-scale on an i7-1185G7
```

  The first line is exact: devices (and how many are compiled Verilog-A instances), the matrix
  dimension split into the deck's nets and the auxiliary rows nothing in the deck names -- a
  branch current per voltage source and inductor, a compiled model's internal nodes *per
  instance*, an `idt` accumulator, one row per denominator degree of a `laplace_*` -- and the
  point count: the exact frequency grid for `.ac`/`.noise`, the swept values for `.dc`, one for
  `.op`, and for `.tran` the card's own `tstop/tstep`, labelled as the floor it is (the step
  controller only adds; 1.0-1.5x on this project's decks).

  The second is a **bracket, and the width is the honest part**. Inside the measured range its
  ends are the neighbouring rows of `bench-scale` -- a statement of fact, not a fit -- and beyond
  the last row they are the exponent-2 and exponent-3 scalings dense LU sits between, which is
  what the user asked for. On top of the solve sits a per-instance model-evaluation term
  (0.006-0.2 ms per compiled instance, 0.002-0.04 ms per non-linear reference primitive), because
  the calibration ladder is linear primitives and cannot speak for a compiled model's cost: at
  3 unknowns the first draft of this estimate was **200x low** on `rectifier.net` for exactly
  that reason. The bracket reaches 30x wide on a small circuit, where Newton iteration counts and
  expression size dominate and no matrix dimension predicts them, and it is gated against the
  five measured decks (`the_bracket_contains_every_measured_deck`) so it cannot silently stop
  covering reality. It covers the solve, not the ~77 ms of start-up and model compilation.

  **`xtask bench-scale` now times `.ac` and `.noise` too**, so the frequency-sweep half of the
  estimate is measured rather than assumed to be some ratio of the transient column. One deck
  serves all four analyses (`AC 1` on the source costs the other three nothing). Measured: an AC
  point costs 1.2-1.5x a transient point despite running no Newton loop, because its arithmetic
  is complex; a noise point is that plus the adjoint.

the previous sample to the new one over the transition time: omitted means the module's
`default_transition`, zero means the negligible `tstep/1000` that `transition` uses. At the
sampling evaluation the ramp has not started, so the output never depends on the current
Newton iterate and its Jacobian contribution is exactly zero -- not an approximation.
Roots follow the LRM (`(1 - r z^-1)`, origin = the factor `z`, hence a pole at the origin is
one sample of delay and an uncancelled zero at the origin is refused as an advance); refused
at build: a lone complex root, `d_0 = 0`, `T <= 0`, `tt < 0`.

  **Gates, closed-form (QSPICE has no LRM Z-filter element):** a unity filter on a 1 V/us
  ramp is the staircase `floor(t/T)` exactly, breakpoints landing on every instant; the
  one-pole IIR `y_k = a y_{k-1} + (1-a) x_k` on a unit step holds `1 - a^(k+1)` after the
  k-th sample to 1e-9; a conjugate pole pair (`zi_np`) against the recurrence
  `y_k = x_k + y_{k-1} - 0.5 y_{k-2}` iterated in the test (overshoot to 2.5 where the old
  fold gave a flat 2.0); AC against `(1-a)/(1 - a e^{-jωT})` across a decade and a half to
  1e-9 in both parts; DC `H(1)`; the realization's root conventions and refusals. Frontend:
  the lowered layout, `default_transition` substitution, argument-count and pair errors.

  **Stated:** the run's t=0 point is the cold-start seed, where a DC source reads 0, so a
  filter with `t0 = 0` samples that; set `t0` inside the first period to sample after the
  sources are up (the test does). The `,,` null zero-argument is not parsed; write the
  no-zeros case in `zi_nd`/`zi_np` form.

Iteration statements now carry the restriction that defines them. `while`/`repeat`/`for` have
run since T2.2; what was missing was LRM 4.5.15 ("Analog operators are not allowed in the
repeat, while and non-genvar for looping statements") and 5.9's list for the same three loops
(no analog filter functions, no event control statements, no contribution statements).
`token-reference.md` had carried the 4.5.15 half as a stated gap since the token audit.

  The LRM's stated reason is literally true here. An operator's state is one slot per `ExprId`
  -- `va_codegen::lower::collect_stateful_calls` walks the *expression arena*, not the statement
  tree -- and a monitored event is one `Module::event_sites` entry registered once at
  elaboration. Neither is per-trip, so a loop running one call site N times does not give it N
  histories: it pushes N signals through one, and every trip but the last is overwritten before
  the step ends.

  **One corpus file was doing exactly that.** `external/verilogaLib-master/adc_16bit_ideal.va`
  writes `for (j = 0; j < 16; j = j + 1) V(out[j]) <+ transition(out_val[j], ...)` over an
  `integer j`: sixteen bits, one `transition` call site, one filter state thrashed sixteen times
  per timepoint, each bit's value depending on the ones evaluated before it. **Corpus 113/132 ->
  112/132** (94/99 self-contained) -- the v0.9.1 shape of drop again, a file that had only ever
  been *building*, and one that started being wrong the day `transition` stopped folding and
  started carrying state. Its fix is one word, `genvar j`, which the diagnostic names; a test
  pins that the advice works, by asserting three trips of a genvar loop unroll into three
  distinct `Builtin::Ddt` arena entries with a state slot each.

  runtime loop, and `@(initial_step)`/`@(final_step)`, which lower to a global flag rather than a
  registered site -- N trips contribute N times and read the same correct flag, which is what the
  source says. A scan of all 171 `external/` files found exactly one contribution inside a
  runtime loop (the `adc` line above, refused for its filter anyway), so enforcing 5.9 literally

0.9.13 - 2026-09-09
-------------------
`$limit`'s model-declared junctions. The value still folds; the junction the access names no
longer does. Closes the third expired-premise item -- and only the half of it that had expired.

  `$limit(V(bi,ei), "pnjlim", VT, Vcrit)` is the corpus's most-used construct (10 files, 72 call
  sites) and it folded away entirely. The *value* fold was right and stays: a converged Newton
  solve is a fixed point of the unlimited equations, so a limiter reshapes the iteration path and
  never the answer, and the stateless `load` ABI holds no previous iterate to limit against.
  What was wrong was throwing the rest away with it. The access argument is not a value at all --
  it names a junction authoritatively, and that is precisely the question `va-codegen` had been
  answering by guessing.

  Its guess was per module and coarse by construction: "this source contains an `exp`, so every
  one of its nodes is a junction." For a compact BJT that is every external terminal behind a
  series resistance, plus the thermal node -- none of which have an exponential across them, all
  of them throttled to roughly vt*ln(...) ~ 0.2 V per Newton iteration.

  So elaboration records both endpoints of each limited branch in
  `va_ir::Module::limited_junctions` (an additive Interface alpha change, sec 6 -- see
  `docs/interfaces.md`'s 2026-09-09 revision), `va-codegen` reports exactly those through
  `ModelInstance::unknown_is_junction`, and `va-core`'s existing `limit_junction` clamp lands
  where the model asked. **Both endpoints**, because `pnjlim` clamps a potential difference while
  the solver limits per unknown; limiting only `bi` of `V(bi,ei)` would leave the difference free
  to take the step the model asked to have bounded. Interface beta is untouched:
  `unknown_is_junction` already existed and already had the right shape, it was only being told
  a guess.

  **A declaration is trusted exclusively, and that is a real trade.** A module that writes at
  least one `$limit` has enumerated its junctions, so the nodes it left out are reported as not
  junctions -- where the blanket guess would have covered them. A model that limits one junction
  and leaves a second exponential unlimited now gets exactly what it asked for. That is the LRM's
  own division of labour, the fix for such a model is the `$limit` it omitted, and no converged
  answer moves either way. A module that writes no `$limit` keeps the guess unchanged.

  **Stated limitations, all costing iteration path and never the answer.** The algorithm name is
  not honoured: `"pnjlim"`, `"fetlim"`, `"typedpnjlim_new"` and any user-written limiter all get
  `convergence::limit_junction`'s `pnjlim`, at `VT_NOMINAL`/`default_vcrit` rather than the
  model's own `vt`/`vcrit` arguments, which are still parsed and never evaluated. A `$limit` on a
  **flow** access declares nothing -- a voltage-shaped clamp on a current unknown is the exact
  bug `unknown_is_junction` was introduced to stop -- and folds as before.

  Gated by `a_model_declared_junction_limits_that_node_and_leaves_the_others_alone`, which
  compiles one model twice, with and without the `$limit`, in a circuit mixing an exponential
  junction with a linear pair driven at 100 V. Declared: it converges. Without: the per-module
  guess claims the 100 V node and Newton's budget runs out. Checked in both directions, not
  assumed -- the failing half is asserted, so the test cannot pass by being decorative.

  Also fixed here, being the premises this invalidated: `va-core/src/convergence.rs`'s header
  still described the clamp as blanket over every unknown (untrue since `unknown_is_junction`
  landed in 2026-07, and doubly so now), and `va_abi::ModelInstance::unknown_is_junction`'s own
  doc still said a generated model opts in "exactly when its source contains an exponential".

  Cost: none measured. `cargo xtask validate` is byte-identical across all 27 gates against the
  previous commit (diffed, not assumed -- no zoo model writes `$limit`, so the fallback path is
  what every gate exercises), and the corpus is steady at 113/132. The three golden-IR snapshots
  gained one `limited_junctions: []` line each and nothing else.

  756 tests, 27/27 golden circuits, corpus 113/132 (95/99 self-contained), clippy and fmt clean.


0.9.12 - 2026-09-07

`$simparam` is answered for real, and answered **during the solve** rather than at elaboration.
Closes the second of the remaining expired-premise items ("v0 has no simulator-parameter store").

  The premise expired at the *evaluation* boundary, not at elaboration, and that distinction is
  the whole design. `iteration` changes on every Newton iteration; `gdev` changes on every
  homotopy stage; the tolerances belong to the solve's configuration, and one module is solved
  many times under different configs. So the values ride `va_abi::AnalysisCtx::sim`
  (`SimParams`, an additive Interface beta change) and are read at load, exactly as `ddt_coeff`
  is. The roadmap's own note said this would need "those values to reach elaboration" -- that
  would have been the wrong fix, and is corrected there now.

  A `parameter` context still folds to the stated default, deliberately: a parameter's value is
  fixed at elaboration by definition and cannot hold a per-iteration quantity.
  `external/bsim6.0.va`'s `parameter real GMIN = $simparam("gmin", 1.0e-15);` keeps working and
  keeps meaning what it meant.

  **Five names are known**: `gdev`, `iteration`, `sourceScaleFactor`, `abstol`, `reltol`. Every
  other name is *unknown*, which is a defined LRM outcome rather than a gap -- it returns the
  query's own default, or, with no default, is refused (LRM 9.18) with the list of names that
  would have worked.

  **`gmin` is deliberately NOT one of them, and this is the correction worth reading.** The first
  cut of this work treated `gmin` and `gdev` as one quantity. The LRM separates them: `gmin` is a
  permanent conductance floor across every nonlinear branch, `gdev` the additional conductance a
  homotopy adds. This engine has only the second, and only while gmin stepping is on, which it is
  not by default. Grepping the corpus decided it rather than argument: **21 occurrences** write
  `$simparam("gmin", 1e-12)` or `$simparam("gmin", 0)` and use the fallback to decide how much
  conductance to add themselves. Answering "known, and it is zero" would have been true of the
  solver and silently stripped the 1e-12 shunt those models add for their own conditioning -- a
  real change to a circuit, made on a technicality. Leaving the name unknown returns each model
  its own fallback, which is both what they mean and what they got before.

  **A correctness consequence in the transient loop, not just a plumbing one.** A model may
  legitimately key its behaviour on the iteration number, so the post-accept evaluation that
  *commits* state has to be made at the iteration the solve actually converged at -- committing
  at 0 would write history from a different model than the one that produced the accepted
  solution. `newton_step` therefore returns a `Solved { x, iterations }` and the commit and
  final-step-probe assembles use it. This is the `StateBuffers` commit-matches-solve invariant,
  extended to a channel that did not exist when it was written.

  Gated by `simparam_iteration_advances_during_a_transient_solve`, whose model is a 1 Ohm
  resistor on iteration 0 and a 1 kOhm one thereafter, so a frozen iteration number lands on an
  RC time constant 1000x wrong. Checked that it discriminates by freezing the number and watching
  it fail, not by assuming. Also `newton_config_and_simparams_agree_on_tolerances`, which pins
  the tolerance constants `va-abi` must duplicate (it is a leaf crate and cannot depend on
  `va-core`) to `NewtonConfig`'s.

  No behaviour change anywhere it is not used: `cargo xtask validate` is byte-identical across
  all 27 gates against the previous commit, and the corpus is steady at 113/132.

0.9.11 - 2026-09-07
-------------------
Instance multiplicity: a deck line's `m=` now means something, and `$mfactor` reports it. Closes
the first of the four remaining 1.0 blockers' expired-premise items -- `$mfactor` folded to `1.0`
because "v0 has no netlist-driven instance parameters", a premise that expired when a device line
gained `name=value` overrides.

  `$mfactor` lowers to `va_ir::Builtin::Mfactor` and is answered at the **instantiation
  boundary** (`Module::multiplicity`), not folded at elaboration -- a module is elaborated once
  and instantiated many times, so no instantiation-dependent value folded there can be right for
  all of them. Same shape `$param_given` took for the same reason.

  **The scaling is applied outside the instance.** LRM 6.3.6's guarantee is that an instance with
  multiplicity m behaves exactly as m identical instances would, and scaling a *stamp* is
  literally that: `va_abi::Multiplied` wraps the built instance and scales residual, Jacobian,
  charge, charge Jacobian, AC excitation, and noise power. One rule for hand-written and
  generated models alike, and it makes two LRM sub-rules fall out rather than needing code:
  a flow probe reads the per-device value (the internal branch unknown is unscaled), and a
  voltage-source-shaped model's constraint row is multiplied through by m, which is the same
  equation with the same root while its KCL entries deliver m times the current.

  Noise power scales as **m, not m^2**: m parallel devices' noise currents are uncorrelated, so
  powers add. Scaling amplitude would be the answer for perfectly correlated sources - the wrong
  physics, and silent, since both produce a plausible spectrum.

  **`m=` does not always mean multiplicity, and that is deliberate.** In the corpus `m` is
  overwhelmingly the junction *grading coefficient*: `external/diode.va`, `diode_basic.va` and
  `angelov_gan.va` each declare `parameter real m = 0.5`. Silently reinterpreting `m=0.5` on such
  a line as "half a device in parallel" would be exactly the class of wrong answer this project
  refuses to produce. So `m=` is the multiplicity **only when the model declares no parameter
  named `m`**, `mult=` is always the multiplicity, and a model declaring `mult` makes the line
  ambiguous and refuses it. No existing deck changes meaning.

  The LRM asks a simulator to warn about double-scaling (6.3.6), and its own two examples are the
  specification, so they are the test: `badres` (`I <+ V/r * $mfactor`) is caught, `parares`
  ($mfactor read in a condition only) is not. Measured on badres with `m=3`: 36 A where 12 A is
  right - exactly m^2, so the warning earns its place. Warning rather than refusal, per the LRM's
  wording and because the model is correct at the default m=1. Stated limit of the *detection*:
  a model that launders `$mfactor` through a variable first slips past, which costs a warning,
  not a wrong answer.

  Not implemented, stated: 6.3.6's rule 4 (potential-referred noise power divided by m) has no
  channel -- `NoiseSink` carries only current sources -- and rule 5 (propagation into
  instantiated submodules) has no inner instance to reach, submodules being flattened by the
  frontend before an instance exists. Built-in R/C/D/V deck lines do not take `m=` yet; this is
  for `X` lines placing a Verilog-A model.

  Costs nothing where it is unused: `Multiplied::wrap` returns the instance untouched at m = 1,
  so a deck with no `m=` is bit-identical -- verified by diffing `cargo xtask validate` against
  the previous commit, all 27 gates byte-for-byte. The one `va-ir` field moved the committed
  golden IR by one line per zoo model, as expected.

Every refusal now says **what** was refused and **why**, in one shape, from whichever layer
raised it. Asked for directly: a user hitting a refusal needs to know what went wrong and why,
and until now the answer depended on which layer said no.

  The worst of it was a mislabelling. A frontend refusal arrived as

    Error: parse error: at preprocessed line 7, column 7 (`@(final_step("tran")) ...`): this
    form of ... is not supported: ..., found Some(Keyword(Keyword("final_step")))

  - one unbroken line, opening with `parse error`, closing with a debug-formatted token. Both
  halves actively mislead: `parse error` says *your source is malformed* when the truth is the
  opposite (the source is valid Verilog-A and this simulator declines to pretend to run it), and
  the `found ...` tail is the shape of an unexpected-token diagnostic, so a correctly spelled
  construct read as a typo. That distinction is the whole point - "my model is wrong" and "this
  simulator will not run my correct model" call for completely different next steps.

    [va-cli] refused: `@(final_step ...)` in a form this engine does not honour
    [va-cli]   where:    at preprocessed line 7, column 7 (`@(final_step("tran")) g = 1.0;`)
    [va-cli]   why:      discarding the trigger would run the body at every solve point instead
    [va-cli]             of when the event fires. For the analysis-filter form that is sharper
    [va-cli]             still: the filter says "not in this analysis", so dropping it runs the
    [va-cli]             body precisely where the model excluded it - wrong in every analysis
    [va-cli]   instead:  write the bare `@(final_step)`, or an `or` list of bare
    [va-cli]             `initial_step`/`final_step`; both are honoured
    [va-cli]   tracking: docs/roadmap.md, section "Analog events"

`@(final_step)` works, and stops being refused in transient. It is `initial_step`'s mirror in
the frontend -- desugared to a guarded `if`, lowered to `va_ir::Builtin::FinalStep`, reading a
new `va_abi::AnalysisCtx::is_final_step` (Interface beta, additive) -- and the entire difficulty
is on the consumer side: a driver knows its first evaluation before making it and its last one
only after the run has ended.

  So the integrator re-solves the last accepted timepoint with the flag set, the same way it
  re-solves a timepoint where a `cross` fired, and for the same reason: a body writing a
  variable that feeds a contribution changes the equations, and recording the pre-body solution
  would report a point at which the model's own statements do not hold. Knowing *which* point
  is last needs no lookahead -- the loop runs `while t < tstop` and clamps its landing to
  `tstop`, so the step being accepted ends the run exactly when its landing time does.

  convergence, so re-solving every run's last point unconditionally would move it by a step's
  worth of round-off in exchange for nothing. Two assembles at the already-accepted point say
  whether any instance's stamps actually change when the flag is set. Cost: two extra `load`
  calls per run. Measured, not asserted: `cargo xtask validate` output is byte-identical to

  `is_final_step` is `true` in DC, AC and noise, so a *swept* DC runs the body at every point.

exists: a signal already past its threshold never crosses it, so a `cross` on it never
fires at all -- the LRM's own sample-and-hold example turns on exactly that. Implementing
only the transient half would have reproduced the bug the construct was invented to avoid.

  So a static solve had to grow events, having had none: va-core gains
  `mna::assemble_with_events`, `newton::solve_with_events` and
  `dc::operating_point_with_events`, all additive -- the existing entry points pass an
  empty set and behave exactly as before. The DC path is two-phase and iterates to a fixed
  point, because which events fire is a property of the solution while the bodies they
  guard change the equations that produce it.

  Interface beta: `monitor`'s `dir`/`tol` arguments folded into one `CrossSpec` carrying
  `at_initialization` too. The method took `dir` at 0.9.2 and `tol` at 0.9.6, so a third
  widening in four versions was the signal to stop widening. `FiredEvents` moved into
  va-abi, both consumers now sharing one shape.

  `above` and `absdelta` were added to RESERVED_WORDS, where LRM Table B.1 has them and
  this lexer did not. Safe: all 24 corpus occurrences of `above` are in comments ("the
  above copyright notice") and `absdelta` appears nowhere.

  Bare form only; `absdelta` and compound triggers remain refused.

  729 tests, 27/27 golden circuits, corpus steady at 113/132, clippy and fmt clean.


0.9.6 — 2026-09-06
------------------
`cross`'s `time_tol`/`expr_tol` are honoured, by a bracketing retry loop. A candidate step
that reveals a crossing it has overshot by more than the requested tolerance is rejected
and re-taken, aiming just past the interpolated crossing, through the same retry path the
LTE controller already uses. Aiming past rather than at the crossing is deliberate: the LRM
requires the event to fire after it, and landing a hair before would hide it.

  `EventSink::monitor` gained a `CrossTol` argument to carry the request. A signature
  change rather than a defaulted companion, for the reason the analysis context was one --
  a consumer that ignores a tolerance is silently failing to honour something the source
  asked for, so no implementor should be able to miss it.

  Measured convergence: ~70 ulps of the timescale -- a 1e-18 s tolerance really is met at
  t = 83us, where f64 spacing is ~1.4e-20 s. Below that spacing it cannot be, and the count
  is reported through `Waveform::unresolved_events` and warned about at the end of the run,
  rather than quietly missed. The blanket compile-time warning from 0.9.5 is gone, being
  no longer true.

  A site that requests no tolerance never brackets, so every pre-existing run is
  bit-identical: 27/27 golden circuits unmoved.

  724 tests, 27/27 golden circuits, corpus steady at 113/132, clippy and fmt clean.


0.9.5 — 2026-09-06
------------------
Stop dropping event arguments. The parser kept only the first two arguments of
`cross`/`timer` and silently discarded the rest, which included `enable` -- whose entire
job is to switch the event off, so a model that disabled an event got it firing anyway.
All arguments now reach elaboration, which interprets them positionally per event kind
and rejects a count the LRM does not define.

  `enable` is honoured: a disabled site registers nothing, so it cannot fire, and it is
  re-evaluated every accepted timepoint since it may switch during a run.

  `cross`'s `time_tol`/`expr_tol` are recorded on the IR and **warned about**. This engine
  fires at the accepted timepoint ending the bracketing step, so resolution is set by the
  timestep rather than by the requested tolerance -- a model asking for 1ns on a 2us step
  gets 2us. Carrying them means the bracketing retry loop that fixes this needs no further
  interface change. A `timer`'s `time_tol` raises no warning: the LRM asks for a timepoint
  within it and the integrator lands exactly, so it is already satisfied.

  Fixed along the way: with `enable` honoured, a disabled site stops reporting, and the
  consumer had been reading "no report" as a value of 0.0 -- which would compare against
  whatever the site last reported and fire the event precisely because it was switched off.
  Unreported slots are now distinct from zero.

  722 tests, 27/27 golden circuits, corpus steady at 113/132, clippy and fmt clean.


0.9.4 — 2026-09-06
------------------
`@(timer(...))` works, and the IR's event sites are unified. `va_ir::Module::cross_sites`
becomes `event_sites: Vec<EventSite>` with `Cross`/`Timer` variants and `Expr::CrossFired`
becomes `EventFired`, so slot numbering is one flat space across event kinds -- the
consumer's fired-flag buffer indexes it and must not care which kind claimed a slot.
Interface beta gains `EventSink::timer(slot, next)`, defaulted to forward to `breakpoint`:
a breakpoint only says "stop here", while a firing must say which event fires there.

  A model re-registers its next occurrence at every accepted timepoint, so it stays
  stateless about its own schedule -- the arithmetic is a pure function of
  (start, period, now). Period defaults to 0, the LRM's "fire once".

  Stated limitations: bare form only (a compound `initial_step or timer(...)` is still
  refused, as are `above` and `absdelta`); an occurrence landing on the run's *initial*
  timepoint is not delivered, that point being a seed rather than a solved step; tolerance
  arguments are parsed and discarded.

  718 tests, 27/27 golden circuits, corpus steady at 113/132, clippy and fmt clean.

0.9.2 — 2026-09-06
------------------
Interface beta gains an event-registration channel (a section 6 coordinated change):

`event_count`/`events`. A model can now tell the transient engine which expressions to
watch for a zero crossing and which absolute times to solve at; `va-transient` polls it

0.9.1 — 2026-09-06
------------------
Event-trigger refusal. `@(...)` triggers other than `initial_step` used to be discarded,
running their body unconditionally -- so `@(cross(V(clk) - vt, 1)) ...` executed at every
